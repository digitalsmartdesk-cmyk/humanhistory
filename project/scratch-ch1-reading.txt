<h2 id="reading">What the figures say about 1925</h2>
<p class="note">These are my reading of the entries and my arithmetic on them, not findings from the sources.</p><div class="pat"><div><h3>In the United States, the premise does not hold as stated</h3><p>By 1925 half of US households had electric power, and the car reached the same share the next year (1926). Radio had passed 10 percent. The 1925 hemline also differs sharply from the 1820s silhouette. Atlantic crossings had fallen from 23–40 days to about 5. I have household data only for the United States, so I cannot say the same of Europe; the dress and crossing rows cover Western fashion and the North Atlantic.</p></div><div><h3>For much of the world it holds better</h3><p>Around 1920 the urban share was about 10 percent in both Africa and Asia, and it reached about 43 and 46 percent by 2016. In 2000, 2 in 10 people still lacked electricity and about half lacked clean cooking fuels. For these regions the large visible shift came after 1925.</p></div><div><h3>The largest change in the second century is in health and numbers, not in objects</h3><p>Life expectancy was about 29 in the early 1800s and 31–32 in 1900, then 72.6–73.2 in 2019–2020. Child deaths before 15 fell from about a quarter in 1950 to 4% in 2020. This is the change a photograph of 1925 would not show.</p></div><div><h3>Both centuries saw the speed of the journey fall by a large factor, in different ways</h3><p>From the 40-day westbound packet to the 1924 Mauretania, about 5 days, the time fell roughly eightfold. From 1924 to the fastest 2020 airliner crossing, about 25-fold. The second figure compares a ship with an aircraft, so it measures a change of method as much as of speed.</p></div><div><h3>What the evidence supports</h3><p>My reading: &#x27;visually similar by 1925&#x27; is better described as &#x27;the shape of modern household life was in place in the United States by 1925, and reached much of the rest of the world after it&#x27;. What changed after 1925 was how many people shared it, how long they lived, and how fast and far they could travel.</p></div></div>
