Power and governance

Seven entries in two groups. Each gives the scale, what the source says about change and how solid the figures are.

Who rules and who votes 4 entries

  1. 1800 to 2025Regimes

    From closed autocracy to electoral democracy and back

    Our World in Data, using the Regimes of the World classification, says almost everyone in 1800 lived under closed autocracies, with about 20 million people in electoral autocracies, which it names as the UK and US. Electoral and liberal democracy spread widely in the 20th century, especially in its second half. The world was at its democratic all-time high in the early 2010s and has fallen since. By 2025 about 1.6 billion people lived in electoral democracies and another 600 million in liberal democracies.

    Totals
    The page says population growth outpaced democratic expansion, so the number of people without democratic rights is higher than ever, with many in China.
    Classification
    RoW reclassified India as an electoral autocracy in 2017, while other sources agree it has become less democratic but still count it as a democracy.

    How solid One classification among several, which the page says sometimes sort countries differently. It gives no share of the world's people in 1900 or 1950, and no count of countries by regime for 2025.

  2. 2025Democracy

    The V-Dem count for 2025

    The V-Dem Institute's 2026 report, as reported, says democracy for the average person has fallen back to the level of 1978. Of 179 countries, 87 are democracies, 31 of them liberal and 56 electoral, and 92 are autocracies, 57 electoral and 35 closed. An estimated 74% of the world's people, about 6 billion, live in autocracies and 26% in democracies. 44 countries, with 41% of the world's population, are autocratizing, and 18, with 5%, are democratizing.

    How solid A news report of V-Dem's report, covering developments through 2025. I did not read the report, and V-Dem's method differs from the Regimes of the World classification above.

  3. 2025Freedom

    Freedom House for 2025

    Freedom House's Freedom in the World 2026 report says global freedom declined for the 20th consecutive year in 2025. 54 countries saw their political rights and civil liberties deteriorate and only 35 improved. 88 countries are rated Free.

    How solid From Freedom House's own press release. It gave no current count of Partly Free and Not Free countries.

  4. 1893 to 2015Women's vote

    When women won the vote

    New Zealand granted women the national vote first, in 1893. The United States did so in 1920, Britain in 1918 for some women and in 1928 on the same terms as men, India in 1921 in two provinces and 1929 in all provinces, and France in 1944. Women could vote at the federal level in Switzerland from 1971 and in Saudi Arabia's local elections from 2015.

    Not voting
    The page lists Vatican City and Eritrea as having no voting, and notes that some countries have suspended national elections.

    How solid From Wikipedia. The dates are for the vote, which in several places was partial or limited by race, region or class. The page gives no count of countries with women's suffrage in any year.

Checks on power 3 entries

  1. 2002 to 2026Press

    Press freedom at a 25-year low

    Reporters Without Borders says the average score of all countries assessed in its 2026 index has never been so low in 25 years. 52.2% of countries and territories are rated difficult or very serious, against 13.7% in 2002, and less than 1% of the world's population lives in a country rated good.

    Count
    52.2% of 180 is about 94 countries, my calculation.

    How solid From RSF's own page. It gives no global average score and no count of countries in each category, and I did not use its journalist-casualty figures.

  2. 2025Corruption

    Perceived corruption

    Transparency International's 2025 index, scored from 0 to 100 with lower meaning more perceived public-sector corruption, gives a global average of 42, the lowest in a decade. 122 of 182 countries score below 50. Denmark is first with 89 for the eighth year, and Somalia and South Sudan share last place with 9. 50 countries have declined since 2012.

    Examples
    Declining examples include the US at 64, Canada at 75 and France at 66, while Estonia (76), South Korea (63) and Bhutan (71) are cited as consistent improvers.

    How solid A law-firm summary of the index. The index measures perceptions of corruption, not corruption itself, and the summary's statement of an 8-point drop in the average looks wrong, so I left it out.

  3. 2011Institutions

    States and the United Nations

    The United Nations now lists 193 member states, the most recent being South Sudan, admitted on 14 July 2011.

    How solid Thin. The page gives the count and the latest member only, and I found no figure for the number of independent states in 1825 or 1925.

Where the record is hard

These four points come from the same sources as the entries above.

Democracy has slipped back

V-Dem says democracy for the average person has fallen to its 1978 level, and OWID says the world peaked in the early 2010s. 44 countries, holding 41% of the world's people, are autocratizing.

What the record shows

These are my reading of the entries, not findings from the sources.

Four independent measures point the same way for recent years

V-Dem, Freedom House, RSF and Transparency International all report decline in the latest year or decade, with different methods and different data. That is agreement in direction, not in size.

Counting countries and counting people give different answers

The world is about evenly split between democracies and autocracies by country, but V-Dem puts 74% of people in autocracies, because the populous countries weigh heavily, with China named by OWID.

The spread of the vote took more than a century

Among the examples, women's suffrage spans 1893 to 2015, and Switzerland's federal vote in 1971 came after France's in 1944.

Classification is part of the story

India was reclassified in 2017 by one system and still counted a democracy by others, so the headline counts depend on whose rules are used.

No series here compares 1925 with now

The regime shares start in 1800 and 2025 and the other indexes begin in 2002 or later, so the two centuries cannot be compared on these figures.

What this chapter cannot show

  • Governance indexes rest on expert judgement and perceptions, and different systems classify the same country differently.
  • The V-Dem, Transparency International and Freedom House figures are partly from news reports and summaries. I read the Freedom House and RSF pages directly but not the full reports.
  • I found no count of independent states, or of democracies, for 1825 or 1925.
  • The chapter does not cover military power, nuclear weapons, state capacity, surveillance or the power of firms, and it does not measure whether people trust their governments.
  • The women's suffrage dates describe the law and not who could vote in practice.
  • The list is seven entries, a selection and not a full account of governance.
