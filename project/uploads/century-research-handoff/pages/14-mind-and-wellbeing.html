Mind and wellbeing

Seven entries in two groups. Each gives the scale, what the source says about change and how solid the figures are.

Burden and care 4 entries

  1. 2023Mental disorders

    How many people live with a mental disorder

    WHO's fact sheet says nearly 1 in 7 people, 1.2 billion, were living with a mental disorder in 2023. That included 470 million with anxiety disorders, 146 million of them children and adolescents, and 322 million with depression, 43 million of them children and adolescents. Among adolescents aged 10 to 19 it counts 3.3 million with bipolar disorder, 4.7 million with eating disorders and 39 million with conduct-dissocial disorder.

    Disability
    WHO's 2025 reports describe anxiety and depression as the second leading cause of long-term disability, with over 1 billion people living with mental health conditions.
    Over a life
    Our World in Data cites an estimate, with no year, that 1 in 3 women and 1 in 5 men will experience major depression in their lives.

    How solid The WHO figures are modelled estimates. The pages give no earlier figures, so they show scale and not change.

  2. 2024 to 2025Care

    Whether people get help

    Most people with mental disorders do not have access to effective care. Only 40% of people with psychosis and one third of people with depression receive formal mental health care. The median government spends just 2% of its total health budget on mental health, according to a September 2025 WHO release citing the Mental Health Atlas 2024. For drug use disorders, the UN report says only about one in five people with serious disorders receive treatment.

    Gaps
    WHO describes stark disparities between high- and low-income countries and a shortage of workforce, but the pages I read give no figures.

    How solid WHO's own pages, plus a news report for the drug figure. The release gave no reference year for the spending figure.

  3. 2021Suicide

    Deaths by suicide

    WHO says 727,000 people take their own life each year, though the same page elsewhere says more than 720,000. In 2021, 73% of suicides, close to three quarters, were in low- and middle-income countries, and suicide was the third leading cause of death among 15 to 29 year olds. WHO says many people are not seeking help and so are not getting the help they need.

    Support
    If this subject is close to you, speaking to a doctor, a trusted person or a local crisis line is a sensible step.

    How solid The page gives no year for the 727,000, no rate per 100,000 and no trend. I did not find a long-run series.

  4. 2022 to 2023Drugs

    Drug use

    The 2025 World Drug Report, as reported, says more than 316 million people used drugs in 2023, 6% of adults aged 15 to 65, up from 5.2% in 2013. About 63 million people had drug use disorders in 2022, and there were about 25 million cocaine users. Fentanyl caused more than 100,000 deaths in the United States in 2023.

    How solid A news report of a UN report. I did not read the report, and I found no figure for drug use before 2013.

Connection and satisfaction 3 entries

  1. 2025Loneliness

    Loneliness and isolation

    WHO's Commission on Social Connection says 1 in 6 people worldwide is affected by loneliness, linked to more than 871,000 deaths a year, about 100 an hour. Between 17% and 21% of people aged 13 to 29 report loneliness, highest among teenagers. About 24% of people in low-income countries report it, about double the 11% in high-income countries.

    Isolation
    Social isolation is estimated to affect up to 1 in 3 older adults and 1 in 4 adolescents, though data on older adults are limited.

    How solid From WHO's own news release. How the 871,000 deaths were attributed is not stated on the page.

  2. 2026Life satisfaction

    How satisfied people say they are

    In the World Happiness Report 2026 Finland ranked first for a record ninth year, Costa Rica fourth, its highest ranking, and Switzerland tenth. Our World in Data says countries with higher average incomes tend to report higher life satisfaction, and that the share saying they are very or rather happy rose in most countries with two or more surveys. In Albania it went from 33.4% in 1998 to 73.9% in 2022, while in Greece satisfaction fell from about 67% in 2007 to 32.4% five years later.

    Measure
    Life satisfaction is the Gallup World Poll's 0 to 10 ladder, from the worst to the best possible life, asked in more than 160 countries.

    How solid The report page gives rankings but no global average, scale or data years, and I did not read the full report. The trend figures are from two survey series that do not cover the whole world.

  3. 2026Young people and screens

    Screens and the well-being of the young

    The World Happiness Report 2026 says heavy social media use appears to be contributing to the drop in well-being among young people in English-speaking countries and Western Europe, especially among girls. WHO's report on social connection also calls for attention to excessive screen time and negative online interactions.

    How solid Tentative. The wording is 'appears to be', so it is an observed association and not a proven cause, and it is limited to some regions.

Where the record is hard

These four points come from the same sources as the entries above.

Most people get no formal care

Only one third of people with depression, 40% of those with psychosis and about one in five with serious drug use disorders receive treatment, and the median government spends 2% of its health budget on mental health.

WHO; UN, via a news report

Loneliness is reported more in poorer countries

About 24% of people in low-income countries report loneliness against about 11% in high-income ones, and WHO describes stark disparities in mental health care between high- and low-income countries.

Loneliness is common among the young

17% to 21% of people aged 13 to 29 report loneliness, highest among teenagers, and the World Happiness Report links heavy social media use to falling well-being among young people in some regions.

Satisfaction can fall quickly

What the record shows

These are my reading of the entries, not findings from the sources.

The scale is large and the care is thin

More than 1 in 7 people live with a mental disorder, and for depression only one third receive formal care while governments spend a median 2% of health budgets on mental health.

The record cannot say whether mental illness has become more common

None of the pages I read gives a long-run series of prevalence, and Our World in Data says many people do not report symptoms, which makes prevalence hard to estimate. The 1925 comparison cannot be made on these figures.

Life satisfaction goes with income and is hit by crises

Across countries higher average income goes with higher satisfaction, it rose in most countries with repeated surveys, and Greece fell sharply after the financial crisis. These are patterns in two survey series, not a cause.

The young are a focus of the 2025 and 2026 reports

WHO and the World Happiness Report both single out young people, for loneliness and for the possible effect of screens, and both are cautious about cause.

Loneliness and care both differ by income

Loneliness is reported about twice as often in low-income countries as in high-income ones, and WHO describes stark gaps in care between them, though the pages give no figures for the care gap.

What this chapter cannot show

  • Mental health is measured here by modelled counts, surveys and self-reports, and many people do not report symptoms.
  • I found no long-run series on mental disorders, suicide, loneliness or life satisfaction, so this chapter cannot compare the century before 1925 with the century after.
  • Several figures are news reports of reports: the World Drug Report and the Happiness Report rankings were not read in full.
  • Life satisfaction is a self-rating on a 0 to 10 ladder, and cultures answer such questions differently.
  • Not covered: sleep, stress at work, dementia, grief, the effect of war and violence, and the experience of people with severe illness.
  • A count of deaths by suicide says nothing about why individuals take their own lives, and I found no rate per 100,000 or share of the poorer countries' population to set the 73% against.
