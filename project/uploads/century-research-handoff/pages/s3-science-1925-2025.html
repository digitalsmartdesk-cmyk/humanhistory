Scientific progress, second century

1925–2025

The century that scaled the foundations and added computing

In 1925 quantum mechanics was still being written down. By 2025 antibiotics, vaccines, and fertilizer had changed who lived, chips and networks had changed how people work, and telescopes, genome sequencers, and neural networks had opened new questions.

1920s 5 milestones

  1. 1926–27Physics

    Quantum mechanics completed

    Erwin Schrödinger, Max Born, Werner Heisenberg

    Schrödinger's wave equation, Born's probability interpretation, and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle gave physics a consistent theory of atoms. It predicts outcomes with great accuracy and underlies chemistry, lasers, and chips.

  2. 1927–29Earth and cosmos

    An expanding universe

    Georges Lemaître, Edwin Hubble

    Lemaître derived, and Hubble measured, that distant galaxies recede faster the farther away they are. Running the expansion backward points to a hot, dense beginning, the idea later called the Big Bang.

  3. 1928Life and medicine

    Penicillin noticed

    Alexander Fleming; St Mary's Hospital, London

    Fleming saw that mould on a culture plate killed nearby bacteria. Howard Florey, Ernst Chain, and colleagues at Oxford purified it and ran the first trials in 1940–41, and wartime production made antibiotics widely available by 1944.

  4. 1928–32Physics

    Antimatter predicted and found

    Paul Dirac, Carl Anderson

    Dirac's relativistic equation for the electron implied a particle with the same mass and opposite charge. Anderson observed the positron in cosmic-ray tracks in 1932, and antimatter is now used in PET scanners.

  5. 1928Physics

    Raman scattering

    C. V. Raman; Calcutta

    Raman found that a small fraction of scattered light changes wavelength, carrying a signature of the molecule it struck. He won the 1930 Nobel Prize in Physics, the first in the sciences for an Asian scientist, and Raman spectroscopy is a standard analytical tool.

1930s 4 milestones

  1. 1931Chemistry and materials

    The chemical bond explained

    Linus Pauling; Caltech

    Pauling applied quantum mechanics to molecules and introduced ideas such as hybridization and electronegativity. Chemistry gained a theory of why atoms bond the way they do.

  2. 1935–38Chemistry and materials

    Nylon and modern polymers

    Wallace Carothers; DuPont

    Carothers' group made nylon, the first fully synthetic fibre, which reached the market in 1938. Polyethylene (1933) and other plastics grew alongside it, and polymer chemistry became a foundation of consumer manufacturing.

  3. 1936Information and mathematics

    Computability

    Alan Turing; Cambridge

    Turing described an abstract machine that can carry out any step-by-step calculation, and showed that some questions cannot be answered by any such procedure. It became the theoretical basis for computers.

  4. 1938–42Physics

    Nuclear fission and the first reactor

    Otto Hahn, Lise Meitner, Enrico Fermi

    Hahn and Fritz Strassmann found that uranium splits, and Meitner and Otto Frisch explained why. Fermi's team started the first controlled chain reaction in Chicago on 2 December 1942. Fission led to nuclear weapons in 1945 and to nuclear power stations from 1954.

1940s 4 milestones

  1. 1944Life and medicine

    DNA carries heredity

    Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, Maclyn McCarty

    The team showed that purified DNA could transform one strain of bacteria into another. It was strong evidence that genes are made of DNA rather than protein.

  2. 1945–46Information and mathematics

    First general-purpose electronic computer

    J. Presper Eckert, John Mauchly; University of Pennsylvania

    ENIAC used about 18,000 vacuum tubes to calculate artillery tables in seconds that took people hours. Stored-program designs followed within a few years.

  3. 1947–58Information and mathematics

    Transistor and integrated circuit

    John Bardeen, Walter Brattain, William Shockley; Jack Kilby, Robert Noyce

    The transistor (Bell Labs, 1947) replaced vacuum tubes, and the integrated circuit (1958–59) put many transistors on one piece of silicon. Chips now hold tens of billions of transistors.

  4. 1948Information and mathematics

    Information theory

    Claude Shannon; Bell Labs

    Shannon defined the bit and showed how much data a channel can carry without error. Digital communication, compression, and error correction all follow from his framework.

1950s 5 milestones

  1. 1953Life and medicine

    Structure of DNA

    James Watson, Francis Crick, Rosalind Franklin, Maurice Wilkins

    Watson and Crick proposed the double helix in April 1953, using X-ray images from Franklin and Wilkins's group. The structure showed how DNA could carry information and copy itself.

  2. 1954Physics

    Silicon solar cell

    Daryl Chapin, Calvin Fuller, Gerald Pearson; Bell Labs

    The first practical photovoltaic cell converted sunlight to electricity at about 6 percent efficiency and was first used on satellites. The cost of solar electricity fell by about 85 percent between 2010 and 2020.

  3. 1955Life and medicine

    Polio vaccine

    Jonas Salk; Pittsburgh

    Salk's inactivated vaccine was declared safe and effective in April 1955, and Albert Sabin's oral vaccine followed in 1961. Wild polio cases have fallen by more than 99 percent since 1988.

  4. 1957–69Earth and cosmos

    Spaceflight

    Soviet and American space programs

    The first satellite (1957), the first human in orbit (1961), and the first Moon landing (1969) opened direct observation of Earth and other worlds. Satellites now supply weather forecasts, navigation, and climate records.

  5. 1958Earth and cosmos

    The Keeling curve

    Charles Keeling; Mauna Loa, Hawaii

    Keeling began continuous measurements of atmospheric carbon dioxide in 1958, at around 315 parts per million. Levels have since passed 420 ppm, and the record underpins the climate assessments of the IPCC, founded in 1988.

1960s 7 milestones

  1. 1960Physics

    The laser

    Theodore Maiman; Hughes Research Laboratories

    Maiman's ruby laser produced the first coherent light beam of its kind. Lasers now drive optical-fibre networks, barcode scanners, eye surgery, and precision manufacturing.

  2. 1944–70Life and medicine

    The Green Revolution

    Norman Borlaug, M. S. Swaminathan, C. Subramaniam

    Short-stemmed, high-yield wheat and rice varieties, together with fertilizer and irrigation, raised harvests sharply. India's wheat output rose from about 12 million tonnes in 1965 to over 70 million by 2000.

  3. 1961–66Life and medicine

    The genetic code

    Marshall Nirenberg, Heinrich Matthaei, Har Gobind Khorana

    Experiments showed that three-letter units of RNA and DNA specify the amino acids that make up proteins. By 1966 the whole code had been deciphered.

  4. 1963–68Earth and cosmos

    Plate tectonics

    Frederick Vine, Drummond Matthews, J. Tuzo Wilson

    Magnetic stripes on the ocean floor showed that new crust forms at mid-ocean ridges and spreads outward. Wegener's drifting continents finally had a mechanism, and earthquakes and volcanoes fell into a pattern.

  5. 1964–65Earth and cosmos

    Afterglow of the Big Bang

    Arno Penzias, Robert Wilson; Holmdel, New Jersey

    A persistent microwave hiss in their antenna turned out to be the cosmic microwave background, radiation released about 380,000 years after the Big Bang. It settled the case for a hot early universe over steady-state rivals.

  6. 1967–80Life and medicine

    Smallpox eradicated

    World Health Organization; Donald Henderson

    An intensified vaccination campaign began in 1967, the last natural case occurred in 1977, and eradication was declared in 1980. Smallpox had killed an estimated 300 million people in the 20th century, and it remains the only human disease eradicated.

  7. 1969–91Information and mathematics

    From ARPANET to the Web

    Vint Cerf, Bob Kahn, Tim Berners-Lee

    The first ARPANET message was sent in 1969, TCP/IP became the network standard in 1983, and Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web at CERN in 1989. The first public website went up in 1991.

1970s 6 milestones

  1. 1971Life and medicine

    Oral rehydration therapy

    Dilip Mahalanabis; refugee camps, West Bengal

    During a cholera outbreak among refugees in 1971, a simple solution of salts and sugar in water cut deaths from around 30 percent to under 4 percent. The Lancet later called it potentially the most important medical advance of the century.

  2. 1971Information and mathematics

    Microprocessor

    Intel; Federico Faggin, Ted Hoff, Masatoshi Shima

    The Intel 4004 put a computer's central processor on one chip. Personal computers, phones, and the controllers inside appliances and cars all follow from it.

  3. 1971–77Life and medicine

    CT and MRI

    Godfrey Hounsfield, Raymond Damadian, Paul Lauterbur, Peter Mansfield

    The first clinical CT scan was made in 1971, and the first MRI scans of the human body followed in the late 1970s. Doctors could see inside the body without surgery.

  4. 1973–83Physics

    The Standard Model

    Many groups; CERN, SLAC, Fermilab

    Quarks, leptons, and the forces among them were assembled into one theory, confirmed by discoveries including neutral currents (1973) and the W and Z bosons (1983). It describes ordinary matter very precisely, though it leaves out gravity, dark matter, and neutrino masses.

  5. 1973–82Life and medicine

    Genetic engineering

    Stanley Cohen, Herbert Boyer, Frederick Sanger, César Milstein

    Recombinant DNA (1973), monoclonal antibodies (1975), and fast DNA sequencing (1977) made genes readable and editable. In 1982, human insulin made by bacteria became the first biotech drug approved.

  6. 1974–87Earth and cosmos

    The ozone layer

    Mario Molina, Sherwood Rowland, Joseph Farman

    Molina and Rowland warned in 1974 that CFCs destroy ozone, the Antarctic ozone hole was reported in 1985, and the Montreal Protocol of 1987 phased CFCs out. The ozone layer is now recovering, an example of research leading to a working treaty.

1980s 3 milestones

  1. 1981–96Life and medicine

    HIV and its treatment

    Françoise Barré-Sinoussi, Luc Montagnier, many clinical teams

    The virus behind AIDS was identified in 1983, and combination antiretroviral therapy from 1996 turned HIV infection into a manageable condition for people with access to treatment.

  2. 1985Life and medicine

    Polymerase chain reaction

    Kary Mullis; Cetus Corporation

    Mullis conceived PCR in 1983, and it was published in 1985. It copies a fragment of DNA millions of times in hours and underlies forensic tests, diagnostics, and sequencing.

  3. 1985–2004Chemistry and materials

    New forms of carbon

    Harold Kroto, Richard Smalley, Robert Curl; Andre Geim, Konstantin Novoselov

    Buckminsterfullerene (C60) was reported in 1985, and graphene, a single layer of carbon atoms, was isolated in 2004. Both opened fields in nanomaterials.

1990s 5 milestones

  1. 1990Earth and cosmos

    Hubble Space Telescope

    NASA, ESA

    Launched in 1990, Hubble imaged deep fields of distant galaxies and, together with other measurements, helped refine the age of the universe to about 13.8 billion years.

  2. 1991Chemistry and materials

    Lithium-ion battery

    John Goodenough, Stanley Whittingham, Akira Yoshino; Sony

    Sony sold the first commercial lithium-ion cells in 1991, building on 1970s and 1980s research that won the 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. They power phones, laptops, and electric vehicles.

  3. 1995Earth and cosmos

    First exoplanet around a Sun-like star

    Michel Mayor, Didier Queloz; Geneva

    51 Pegasi b showed that planets orbit other Sun-like stars. Astronomers have since confirmed more than 5,000 planets beyond the solar system.

  4. 1996–97Life and medicine

    Cloning from an adult cell

    Ian Wilmut, Keith Campbell; Roslin Institute

    Dolly the sheep was born in 1996 and announced in 1997. She showed that the nucleus of a specialized adult cell can be reprogrammed to build a whole animal.

  5. 1998Earth and cosmos

    Accelerating expansion and dark energy

    Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt, Adam Riess

    Distant supernovae appeared fainter than expected, meaning the universe's expansion is speeding up. Dark energy accounts for roughly two-thirds of the universe's energy content, and its nature is unknown.

2000s 1 milestone

  1. 2001–03Life and medicine

    The human genome sequenced

    International Human Genome Project, Celera

    Drafts appeared in 2001 and the project was declared complete in 2003, with a gapless version following in 2022. The cost of sequencing a genome has fallen from roughly $100 million to under $1,000.

2010s 5 milestones

  1. 2012Physics

    Higgs boson

    ATLAS and CMS collaborations; CERN

    The Large Hadron Collider teams announced the particle on 4 July 2012, confirming the mechanism that gives elementary particles mass. Peter Higgs and François Englert shared the 2013 Nobel Prize in Physics.

  2. 2012Life and medicine

    CRISPR gene editing

    Jennifer Doudna, Emmanuelle Charpentier

    They showed that the bacterial enzyme Cas9 can be programmed to cut DNA at a chosen site. A CRISPR therapy for sickle cell disease was approved in the UK and US in late 2023.

  3. 2012–22Information and mathematics

    Deep learning and language models

    Geoffrey Hinton, Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever, Ashish Vaswani, others

    AlexNet's win in an image-recognition contest in 2012 launched deep learning, the Transformer architecture appeared in 2017, and chat systems reached the public in 2022. The 2024 Nobel Prizes in Physics and Chemistry both recognized work in this area.

  4. 2014–23Earth and cosmos

    India's planetary missions

    ISRO; Mars Orbiter Mission, Chandrayaan-3

    In 2014 India became the first country to reach Mars orbit on its first attempt, and in 2023 Chandrayaan-3 landed near the Moon's south pole.

  5. 2015–16Physics

    Gravitational waves detected

    LIGO Scientific Collaboration; Livingston and Hanford

    LIGO recorded ripples in spacetime from two black holes merging about 1.3 billion light-years away on 14 September 2015. Einstein had predicted such waves a century earlier.

2020s 4 milestones

  1. 2020Life and medicine

    AlphaFold

    DeepMind; Demis Hassabis, John Jumper

    AlphaFold 2 predicted protein structures at close to experimental accuracy in a 2020 benchmark, and a database covering more than 200 million proteins followed in 2022. The work shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

  2. 2020–21Life and medicine

    mRNA vaccines

    Katalin Karikó, Drew Weissman, BioNTech, Moderna

    Modified-nucleoside work from 2005 made mRNA usable as a drug. COVID-19 vaccines built on it were authorized in December 2020, less than a year after the virus's genome was published. Karikó and Weissman won the 2023 Nobel Prize in Medicine.

  3. 2021–22Earth and cosmos

    James Webb Space Telescope

    NASA, ESA, CSA

    Launched on 25 December 2021, Webb released its first images in July 2022. Its infrared view reaches galaxies from within a few hundred million years of the Big Bang and analyzes the atmospheres of exoplanets.

  4. 2022Physics

    Laboratory fusion ignition

    Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory

    On 5 December 2022, the National Ignition Facility produced more fusion energy than the laser energy delivered to the target, about 3.15 megajoules from 2.05. The facility as a whole still used far more energy to run, so practical fusion power remains far off.

Context and limits

Who got the credit

Rosalind Franklin's X-ray work was central to the double helix, and she died before the 1962 Nobel Prize. Jocelyn Bell Burnell found the first pulsars in 1967, but the 1974 Nobel went to her supervisor and a colleague. Credit in large collaborations, such as LIGO and the LHC, is spread over thousands of people.

What it cost and what is open

Nuclear arsenals peaked at about 70,000 warheads in the mid-1980s. Atmospheric CO2 has passed 420 ppm. Bacterial resistance to antibiotics was directly responsible for about 1.2 million deaths in 2019, by one major estimate. The social effects of AI and gene editing are being worked out as they happen.

How settled the recent entries are

Historians need distance to judge what mattered. Items from the last two decades are included because they are important now, and some will look smaller or larger with time.
