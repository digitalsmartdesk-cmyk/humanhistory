Evidence brief: the living world

Nature defended

Eleven defenses of species, land, forests and oceans, and where they fall short

Protected areas, a whaling ban, the High Seas Treaty, forest monitoring, restoration law and the money behind them. Each entry says what exists, what the evidence shows it has done and how solid that evidence is. A section near the end lists where the defenses are thinning.

Figures current to 13 October 2026. The framework's global review opens on 19 October, so its verdict is not yet in.

The defenses

Eleven entries in five areas. The date is when the system began or when the result was reported.

Protected space 3 entries

  1. 2022 to 2030Framework

    Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework

    The framework has 23 global targets, including the aim of protecting at least 30 percent of land and ocean by 2030. By the August 2026 meetings in Nairobi, 172 Parties, almost 90 percent, had submitted national targets, and 130 had submitted national reports. The first global review of progress is due at COP17, 19 to 30 October 2026, in Yerevan.

    Verdict so far
    The Executive Secretary told the Nairobi meeting that fewer than half of aligned national targets assessed by Parties are currently on track.
    Largest gaps
    Mobilizing biodiversity finance, reforming harmful subsidies, addressing business impacts and dependencies, and shifting toward sustainable consumption.

    How solid From the Convention's own press releases. The 2022 adoption date is general knowledge. The review at COP17 had not happened when I wrote this.

  2. 2024Report

    Protected areas

    The Protected Planet Report 2024 puts coverage at 17.6 percent of land and inland waters and 8.4 percent of ocean and coastal areas. Since 2020, 629,000 square kilometres of land and 1.77 million square kilometres of sea have been added. 51 countries and territories exceed 30 percent on land, and 31 exceed it at sea.

    To reach 30%
    Another 16.7 million square kilometres (12.4 percent) of land and 78.3 million (21.6 percent) of sea.
    Quality
    Only 8.52 percent of land is both protected and connected. Only 5.7 percent of the ocean is in marine protected areas that are implemented or actively managed, and 2.8 percent is fully or highly protected.
    Key areas
    Over two-thirds of key biodiversity areas are partially or fully covered, and the remaining 32 percent lie entirely outside protected areas.

    How solid From the report's executive summary, a UN-backed dataset of official submissions. Coverage is counted by area, so it does not show whether protection is enforced.

  3. 2025 / 2026Treaty

    High Seas Treaty

    The Agreement on Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction passed 60 ratifications on 20 September 2025, with Sierra Leone and Morocco the last two to deposit. It entered into force on 17 January 2026. Palau was the first to ratify, on 22 January 2024.

    Allows
    Marine protected areas beyond national jurisdiction, environmental impact assessments, and rules on access to and benefit sharing from marine genetic resources.

    How solid IUCN's announcement. I found no marine protected area yet created under the treaty, and the page does not give the share of ocean the treaty covers.

Species 2 entries

  1. 1986 to 2019Recovery

    Whaling ban and the humpback whale

    Commercial whaling was curbed and then banned in 1986. Humpbacks in the western South Atlantic fell to a low of 440 in 1958, from about 27,000 before hunting. A 2019 study estimates about 25,000 now, roughly 93 percent of the pre-hunt level.

    Source
    Zerbini and colleagues, Royal Society Open Science, published online 16 October 2019.

    How solid One population of one species, reported by Science News. Other whale populations recovered less, and the ban is only one factor.

  2. 1993 to 2020Study

    Extinctions prevented

    Bolam and colleagues estimate that conservation action prevented at least 28 bird and mammal extinctions since 1993: 21 to 32 birds and 7 to 16 mammals. They drew on 137 experts.

    Against
    In the same period 15 bird and mammal species went extinct or are strongly suspected to have done so. The vaquita is still declining quickly.

    How solid The figures are estimates of what would have happened without conservation, not counts. The study covers birds and mammals only.

Forests and land 2 entries

  1. 2025 to 2026Satellite alerts

    Amazon deforestation alerts

    Brazil's DETER system recorded 2,874.38 square kilometres of deforestation alerts from August 2025 to July 2026, down from 4,495 in the previous period. That is the lowest since the series began in 2016 and 55.6 percent below the average of the last ten cycles.

    How solid A report in Agência Brasil of figures from the national space research institute. The article says the fall was 36.87 percent, but the two areas it gives imply about 36.06 percent, and it does not explain the gap. Alerts are not the same as the annual official count.

  2. 2024 to 2050Law

    EU Nature Restoration Law

    The law took effect on 18 August 2024. Member States must have restoration measures in place on at least 20 percent of the EU's land and 20 percent of its sea by 2030, and for all ecosystems that need restoration by 2050.

    Plans
    Draft national restoration plans are due two years after entry into force, so about August 2026, with reviews by 2032 and 2042.
    Rivers
    The law aims to restore at least 25,000 kilometres of rivers by 2030.

    How solid From the European Commission's announcement. I did not check whether Member States met the 2026 deadline for draft plans.

Oceans and chemistry 3 entries

  1. 1987 to 2066Treaty

    Ozone layer and the Montreal Protocol

    The phase-out covers nearly 99 percent of banned ozone-depleting substances. If current policies stay, the ozone layer is expected to return to 1980 levels by about 2066 over the Antarctic, 2045 over the Arctic and 2040 elsewhere. In the 2026 Planetary Health Check, stratospheric ozone is one of two boundaries that are not transgressed.

    How solid The WMO page and a summary of the 2026 check. The 1987 date is general knowledge. Ozone and aerosols are the two boundaries that have improved over the last decade.

  2. 2024Management

    Fisheries

    Wild catch has stayed in a band of 86 to 94 million tonnes since the late 1980s, about 92 million tonnes in 2024, while farmed aquatic animal production passed 100 million tonnes. 62.4 percent of assessed stocks are fished sustainably.

    Trend
    The sustainable share fell 2.1 points from the 2024 report.

    How solid FAO's 2026 report through Mongabay. A steady catch can reflect management or limits on stocks, and the report's stock count changed from about 500 to 2,665 between editions.

  3. 2026Governance

    Deep-sea mining held back

    At the International Seabed Authority's two-week Council session reported on 20 March 2026, no mining was approved. The Mining Code, the rulebook needed before commercial extraction, remains unfinished. 40 countries back a moratorium.

    Pressure
    Concern about unilateral mining outside the Authority, centred on a Nauru-linked subsidiary of The Metals Company. A report on it was due at the July meeting.

    How solid A report in Oceanographic Magazine. I did not find the outcome of the July meeting, so this describes the position in March.

Money 1 entry

  1. 2019 to 2030Finance

    Funding for nature

    International finance to developing countries was $29.8 billion in 2023 for biodiversity-related spending and $16.8 billion for biodiversity-specific spending, against a target of $20 billion a year by 2025. The 2030 target is $30 billion a year.

    Wider targets
    $200 billion a year from all sources by 2030, and $500 billion of harmful subsidies reduced or reformed.
    Gap
    A $700 billion annual biodiversity funding gap by 2030.
    Nature-based solutions
    $220 billion of total annual flows in 2023, up 11 percent on 2022, of which $23.4 billion was private.

    How solid From a UK government finance dashboard. The page gives no current total for the $200 billion target or for subsidies, so I cannot say how close either is.

Where defenses are thinning

These are gaps reported in the sources or plain readings of them. Where I could not find what happened next, the card says so.

The framework's own review shows slippage

The Executive Secretary says fewer than half of aligned national targets assessed are on track, with the largest gaps in finance, harmful subsidies, business impacts and consumption.

Source: CBD press release, August 2026 (P)

Protection is mostly on paper

17.6 percent of land and 8.4 percent of ocean are covered, but only 5.7 percent of the ocean is actively managed and only 2.8 percent strictly protected. Only 8.52 percent of land is protected and connected, and 32 percent of key biodiversity areas lie entirely outside protected areas.

Source: Protected Planet Report 2024 (P)

The money is short

Biodiversity-specific international finance was $16.8 billion in 2023, below the $20 billion target for 2025, and the page cites a $700 billion annual funding gap by 2030. The wetlands report puts the cost of reaching conservation goals for wetlands alone at $275 billion to $550 billion a year.

Source: UK finance dashboard (P); Mongabay on wetlands (S)

The plastics treaty is deadlocked

Negotiations in Geneva from 5 to 15 August 2025 ended with no agreement. A later session on 7 February (the page gives no year; I read it as 2026) was mainly administrative and elected a new chair, and I found no date for the next one. States disagree on ambition and on whether chemicals in plastics are covered.

Source: Wikipedia, Global plastic pollution treaty (S)

The deep seabed has no rules yet

The Mining Code is unfinished and 40 countries back a moratorium, but concern about unilateral mining continues. The 2026 Red List update says 125 of 201 endemic vent molluscs are threatened by deep-sea mining.

Source: Oceanographic Magazine (S); Chronicle India (S)

Tropical forest loss is still high

Loss of tropical primary forest fell 36 percent in 2025 but is still 46 percent higher than a decade ago, and fire caused 42 percent of global tree cover loss. A fall in Amazon alerts does not show the same for other forests.

Source: WRI and Global Forest Watch (P); last sentence is my reading

Nothing here addresses ocean heat

The coral bleaching event reached 84 percent of reef area and a tipping point is said to be passing. None of the defenses listed acts on ocean heat itself. The emissions path is covered in the chapter on defenses against catastrophe.

Source: NOAA (P), Stockholm Resilience Centre (P); last two sentences are my reading

Wetlands are still being lost

The Global Wetland Outlook says about a quarter of remaining wetlands are degraded and destroyed at 0.52 percent a year, and that up to 20 percent of wetlands could be lost in the next 25 years. It says protecting wetlands is far less expensive than restoring them.

Source: Mongabay on the Global Wetland Outlook 2025 (S)

What the defenses have in common

These are my reading of the entries, not findings from the sources.

Defenses work best on a single, countable cause

The whaling ban, the ozone chemicals and the Amazon alerts each target one act that can be monitored. The recoveries in this chapter are of that kind.

Targets are ahead of delivery

The 30 percent targets stand against 17.6 and 8.4 percent coverage and much smaller shares that are managed or strictly protected.

Diffuse drivers have no comparable instrument

Land-use change, consumption, plastic and ocean heat are spread across many actors. For these the entries describe frameworks and negotiations, and I found no measured turnaround.

Recovery is slow and species-specific

The humpback population reached about 93 percent of its pre-hunt size roughly 33 years after the ban, and the same studies show other species still falling. Conservation prevented at least 28 extinctions while 15 others occurred.

What this chapter cannot show

  • The list is my selection. Eleven entries cannot cover the many local and national defenses, and I did not cover freshwater protection, soil, invasive species control or national endangered species laws.
  • Cause is hard to show. A rise in a population or fall in deforestation may reflect enforcement, prices, weather or all three, and the sources say so in only some cases.
  • Several items are early. The framework's global review, the July seabed meeting and the national restoration plans were still pending or unchecked when I wrote this.
  • Coverage counts area, not outcome. A protected area that is not managed is counted in the 17.6 and 8.4 percent.
  • Most figures reached me through summaries, so they need checking against the original reports before use.
