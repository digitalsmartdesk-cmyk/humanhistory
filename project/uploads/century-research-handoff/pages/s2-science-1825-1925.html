Scientific progress, first century

1825–1925

The century that built the physical and biological foundations

In 1825 the first public steam railway was opening. By 1925 electricity powered cities, X-rays and radio were routine, germs and genes had reshaped medicine and biology, and relativity and quantum theory had rewritten physics.

1820s 2 milestones

  1. 1828Chemistry and materials

    Urea made from inorganic materials

    Friedrich Wöhler; Göttingen

    Wöhler produced urea, a compound found in living bodies, from ammonium cyanate. The result weakened the belief that organic matter needs a special vital force, and it opened organic synthesis as a field.

  2. 1829–32Information and mathematics

    Non-Euclidean geometry

    Nikolai Lobachevsky, János Bolyai

    Both men showed that consistent geometries exist in which parallel lines behave differently from Euclid's rule. Gauss had reached similar ideas privately. The mathematics later supplied the language for curved space in general relativity.

1830s 4 milestones

  1. 1830–33Earth and cosmos

    Deep time

    Charles Lyell; Britain

    Principles of Geology argued that the Earth was shaped by slow processes still operating today, such as erosion and the buildup of sediment. That implied an immensely old planet and gave Darwin the time scale he needed.

  2. 1831Physics

    Electromagnetic induction

    Michael Faraday; Royal Institution, London

    Faraday showed that a changing magnetic field drives a current in a nearby wire, and Joseph Henry in the United States found the effect independently. Every electric generator and transformer rests on it.

  3. 1838Earth and cosmos

    First distance to a star

    Friedrich Bessel; Königsberg

    By measuring the tiny shift in the position of 61 Cygni as the Earth orbits the Sun, Bessel found that it lies roughly 10 light-years away. Stellar parallax gave astronomy its first yardstick beyond the solar system.

  4. 1839Information and mathematics

    Photography announced

    Louis Daguerre, Nicéphore Niépce; France

    Daguerre's process produced sharp images on silvered plates, following Niépce's first surviving photograph of about 1826. Light became a recording tool, and scientists soon used plates to capture spectra, microbes, and the sky.

1840s 6 milestones

  1. 1843Information and mathematics

    The first published program

    Ada Lovelace, Charles Babbage; London

    Lovelace's notes on Babbage's Analytical Engine included an algorithm for Bernoulli numbers and the idea that such a machine could manipulate any symbols, not only numbers. The engine itself was never built.

  2. 1843–47Physics

    Conservation of energy

    James Joule, Julius Mayer, Hermann Helmholtz

    Joule measured how much mechanical work produces a given amount of heat, and others stated the general principle that energy changes form but is never lost. It became the first law of thermodynamics.

  3. 1844Information and mathematics

    Electric telegraph

    Samuel Morse, Alfred Vail; United States

    Morse sent the first long-distance message, from Washington to Baltimore, building on earlier British systems. Messages began to outrun any messenger, and the first lasting transatlantic cable in 1866 linked two continents.

  4. 1846Life and medicine

    Surgical anesthesia

    William Morton; Massachusetts General Hospital

    On 16 October a public demonstration showed that ether could keep a patient unconscious during an operation. The method spread across Europe within months, and surgeons no longer had to finish in minutes.

  5. 1846Earth and cosmos

    Neptune found by calculation

    Urbain Le Verrier, John Couch Adams, Johann Galle

    Irregularities in the orbit of Uranus led two mathematicians to predict another planet. Galle found it in Berlin within about a degree of Le Verrier's position, a showpiece for Newtonian mechanics.

  6. 1847–67Life and medicine

    Handwashing and antisepsis

    Ignaz Semmelweis, Joseph Lister

    Semmelweis sharply cut deaths from childbed fever in a Vienna hospital ward by having doctors wash with chlorinated lime. Lister later applied carbolic acid to wounds and instruments. Surgery moved from a last resort toward a controlled procedure.

1850s 5 milestones

  1. 1848–65Physics

    Thermodynamics and entropy

    William Thomson (Kelvin), Rudolf Clausius

    Kelvin defined an absolute temperature scale, and Clausius stated the second law and coined the term entropy. Together they explained why heat engines have limits and why processes run in one direction.

  2. 1854Life and medicine

    Cholera traced to a water pump

    John Snow; Soho, London

    Snow plotted cholera deaths on a map and traced them to a single pump on Broad Street. His method helped found epidemiology, and it came decades before Koch isolated the cholera bacterium in 1883.

  3. 1856Chemistry and materials

    Synthetic dye

    William Perkin; London

    Aged 18, Perkin accidentally made the purple dye mauveine while trying to synthesize quinine from coal tar. He built a factory to produce it, and the dye and drug industries grew from the same chemistry.

  4. 1859Physics

    Spectroscopy

    Gustav Kirchhoff, Robert Bunsen; Heidelberg

    Each chemical element emits and absorbs light at its own set of wavelengths. Chemists used this to find new elements, and astronomers began reading the composition of the Sun and stars from their light.

  5. 1859Life and medicine

    Natural selection

    Charles Darwin, Alfred Russel Wallace

    On the Origin of Species set out evidence that species change over generations through inherited variation and differential survival. Wallace had reached the same idea independently in 1858. Biology gained a single organizing theory.

1860s 4 milestones

  1. 1861–85Life and medicine

    Germ theory

    Louis Pasteur, Robert Koch

    Pasteur disproved spontaneous generation, Koch linked specific bacteria to anthrax (1876) and tuberculosis (1882), and Pasteur's rabies vaccine followed in 1885. Disease had identifiable causes, so sanitation, sterilization, and vaccines could be targeted.

  2. 1865Life and medicine

    Laws of inheritance

    Gregor Mendel; Brno

    Mendel crossed thousands of pea plants and found that traits pass on as discrete units in predictable ratios. He presented the work in 1865. It went largely unnoticed until 1900.

  3. 1865Physics

    Electromagnetic theory of light

    James Clerk Maxwell; Britain

    Maxwell's equations unified electricity, magnetism, and light, and predicted electromagnetic waves traveling at the speed of light. They underlie radio, radar, and all of electrical engineering.

  4. 1869Chemistry and materials

    Periodic table

    Dmitri Mendeleev; St Petersburg

    Mendeleev arranged the known elements by atomic weight and chemical behavior, leaving gaps. He predicted the properties of missing elements, and gallium (1875), scandium, and germanium later matched.

1870s 2 milestones

  1. 1876Information and mathematics

    Telephone

    Alexander Graham Bell; Boston

    Bell's patent turned speech into a varying electric current and back again. Within decades, exchanges and long-distance lines created the first real-time voice networks.

  2. 1879–82Physics

    Practical electric light and power

    Thomas Edison, Joseph Swan; New York, London

    Long-lasting incandescent lamps and central power stations, such as Pearl Street in New York in 1882, made electricity a service sold to homes and factories. The whole system rested on Faraday and Maxwell's earlier work.

1880s 2 milestones

  1. 1887Physics

    Radio waves produced

    Heinrich Hertz; Karlsruhe

    Hertz generated and detected electromagnetic waves in the laboratory, confirming Maxwell's prediction. He saw no practical use for them. Wireless telegraphy was built within a decade.

  2. 1887Physics

    No ether detected

    Albert Michelson, Edward Morley; Cleveland

    Their interferometer found no difference in the speed of light along and across the Earth's motion. The null result undermined the idea of a light-carrying ether and became key background for relativity.

1890s 5 milestones

  1. 1895Physics

    X-rays

    Wilhelm Röntgen; Würzburg

    Röntgen noticed a new penetrating radiation that could image bones, and X-ray images were in hospital use within months. He received the first Nobel Prize in Physics, in 1901.

  2. 1895–1901Information and mathematics

    Wireless telegraphy

    Jagadish Chandra Bose, Guglielmo Marconi

    Bose demonstrated millimetre-wave radio signals in Calcutta in 1895, and Marconi reported a transatlantic signal in 1901. Wireless let ships and, later, broadcasters communicate without wires.

  3. 1896–98Physics

    Radioactivity

    Henri Becquerel, Marie and Pierre Curie

    Becquerel found that uranium emits radiation spontaneously. The Curies isolated polonium and radium, and Marie Curie went on to win Nobel Prizes in physics (1903) and chemistry (1911). Atoms were no longer thought unchanging.

  4. 1896Earth and cosmos

    Carbon dioxide and climate

    Svante Arrhenius; Stockholm

    Arrhenius calculated that changing the amount of CO2 in the atmosphere would change surface temperature, and that burning coal could warm the planet over time. He expected any warming to be slow and possibly welcome.

  5. 1897Physics

    The electron

    J. J. Thomson; Cambridge

    Thomson showed that cathode rays are streams of particles far lighter than hydrogen atoms. The electron was the first subatomic particle found, and it is the basis of electronics.

1900s 5 milestones

  1. 1900Physics

    The quantum

    Max Planck; Berlin

    To explain the spectrum of heat radiation, Planck assumed that energy is exchanged in small discrete amounts. He treated it as a mathematical device at first. It opened the road to quantum theory.

  2. 1900Life and medicine

    Mendel rediscovered

    Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns, Erich von Tschermak

    Three botanists independently reached results matching Mendel's and found his earlier paper. Heredity became an experimental science, and the word genetics was coined a few years later.

  3. 1905Physics

    Einstein's miracle year

    Albert Einstein; Bern

    Four papers explained the photoelectric effect with light quanta, gave evidence for atoms through Brownian motion, introduced special relativity, and derived the equivalence of mass and energy. Space, time, and light were redefined.

  4. 1907Chemistry and materials

    First fully synthetic plastic

    Leo Baekeland; New York

    Bakelite, made from phenol and formaldehyde, set permanently under heat and insulated against electricity. It started the plastics industry.

  5. 1909–13Chemistry and materials

    Nitrogen from air

    Fritz Haber, Carl Bosch; Karlsruhe, Ludwigshafen

    Haber made ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen under pressure, and Bosch scaled it up for the BASF plant that opened in 1913. Synthetic fertilizer now supports a large share of the world's food, and the same process supplied explosives.

1910s 7 milestones

  1. 1910–15Life and medicine

    Genes on chromosomes

    Thomas Hunt Morgan; Columbia University

    Morgan's fruit-fly group showed that genes sit in a linear order on chromosomes and that linked genes are inherited together. Mendel's abstract factors now had a physical home.

  2. 1911–13Physics

    The nuclear atom

    Ernest Rutherford, Niels Bohr; Manchester, Copenhagen

    Rutherford's team found that an atom's mass is concentrated in a tiny nucleus, and Bohr explained hydrogen's spectrum with electrons in fixed orbits. Atomic structure became something that could be calculated.

  3. 1912Earth and cosmos

    Standard candles

    Henrietta Leavitt; Harvard College Observatory

    Leavitt found that brighter Cepheid variable stars pulse more slowly, so their period reveals their true brightness. The relationship turned Cepheids into tools for measuring distances to other galaxies.

  4. 1912Earth and cosmos

    Continental drift proposed

    Alfred Wegener; Marburg

    Wegener argued that the continents were once joined, citing matching coastlines, fossils, and rock formations. Most geologists rejected it for lack of a mechanism, and plate tectonics vindicated the core idea in the 1960s.

  5. 1913–14Information and mathematics

    Ramanujan's letters

    Srinivasa Ramanujan, G. H. Hardy; Madras, Cambridge

    Ramanujan, largely self-taught, sent Hardy pages of formulas on infinite series, partitions, and number theory. He moved to Cambridge in 1914 and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1918. His notebooks hold nearly 4,000 results.

  6. 1915–19Physics

    General relativity confirmed

    Albert Einstein, Arthur Eddington

    Einstein described gravity as curved spacetime in 1915. Eddington's 1919 eclipse observations found starlight bending by close to the predicted amount, which made the theory famous worldwide.

  7. 1918Information and mathematics

    Noether's theorem

    Emmy Noether; Göttingen

    Noether proved that every continuous symmetry of a physical system corresponds to a conserved quantity, such as energy from symmetry in time. The result is central to modern physics.

1920s 4 milestones

  1. 1921–22Life and medicine

    Insulin

    Frederick Banting, Charles Best, J. B. Collip, J. J. R. Macleod; Toronto

    Extracts from animal pancreas were first given to a 14-year-old boy with diabetes in January 1922. A condition that had been quickly fatal became manageable.

  2. 1923–25Earth and cosmos

    Galaxies beyond our own

    Edwin Hubble; Mount Wilson Observatory

    Using Leavitt's method on a Cepheid in the Andromeda nebula, Hubble showed that it lay far outside the Milky Way. The result was presented in January 1925, and the universe turned out to hold many galaxies.

  3. 1924–25Physics

    Quantum mechanics takes shape

    Louis de Broglie, S. N. Bose, Wolfgang Pauli, Werner Heisenberg

    De Broglie proposed matter waves, Bose derived Planck's law by counting photon states (Einstein extended it into Bose–Einstein statistics), Pauli stated his exclusion principle, and Heisenberg published matrix mechanics. Schrödinger and Dirac completed the theory within three years.

  4. 1925Earth and cosmos

    What stars are made of

    Cecilia Payne; Harvard

    Payne's doctoral thesis showed that stars are overwhelmingly hydrogen and helium, not Earth-like mixtures. Senior astronomers at first doubted it, and it is now basic astrophysics.

Context and limits

Who got the credit

Faraday and Henry, Darwin and Wallace, and Bell and Gray all worked in parallel, and credit often went to whoever published or patented first. Mendel went unnoticed for 35 years. Lovelace, Leavitt, Curie, Payne, and Noether made central contributions while working with limited recognition or pay.

Where the record comes from

The best-documented science of this century came from Europe and North America. Bose and Ramanujan worked and were recognized within a European-dominated system, and many other contributors elsewhere are poorly recorded.

What it cost

Coal-fired industry polluted cities and began the rise in atmospheric CO2. Steamships, telegraphs, and quinine helped European empires expand, and biology was misused to justify racism and eugenics.
