The gains

Eight entries in three groups. Each gives the scale, the cause where the source gives one, and how solid the figures are.

Food 3 entries

  1. 1961 to 2023Food supply

    More calories for each person

    The FAO's measure of food supplied per person per day rose from 2,184 kilocalories in 1961 to 2,457 in 1980, 2,659 in 2000, 2,796 in 2010 and 3,006 in 2023. Supply grew substantially in Asia and Africa, and the regional gaps have been converging. In France the average food supply more than doubled over the last 300 years.

    Not the same as eating
    The figure counts food available at retail level and leaves out waste in homes, so people eat less than the supply. It measures energy only, not vitamins or minerals.

    How solid The world figures are from the FAO series as compiled by Our World in Data. The page gives no regional values in its text and the French long-run figure is the only one before 1961.

  2. 19th and 20th centuriesFamine

    Famines and their deaths

    Ó Gráda calls 100 million deaths a conservative estimate for famines in the 19th century, about the same as the 20th-century total, so the 19th century was far deadlier in proportion. The deadliest 20th-century famine was in China from 1958 to 1962, an estimated 36 million deaths, or 45 million in Dikötter's estimate. Wartime famines in the Second World War killed about 25 million.

    Since
    The death rate from famine fell to very low levels in the second half of the 20th century. Northeast Nigeria in 2016–2019, with an estimated 340,000 deaths, is the only large famine in a democracy since 1870, according to the page.

    How solid Estimates differ widely: the Chinese toll is 36 or 45 million depending on the author. The page cites Devereux as calling the 20th century the highest total for any century, which sits oddly beside Ó Gráda's figure, and it gives no 21st-century total.

  3. 2022 to 2024Hunger

    People still without enough to eat

    The UN estimates 8.2% of the world, about 673 million people, faced hunger in 2024, with a range of 638 to 720 million. The share was 8.5% in 2023 and 8.7% in 2022. In Africa it was over 20%, affecting 307 million people, while in Asia it fell from 7.9% in 2022 to 6.7%, or 323 million, in 2024.

    Outlook
    512 million people could be chronically undernourished by 2030, almost 60% of them in Africa. The UN goal is to end hunger by 2030.

    How solid The 2024 figure is a UN estimate with a stated range. The page gives no hunger figure for 2000 or 2019, so I cannot show the long run.

Poverty and income 3 entries

  1. 1820 to 2015Extreme poverty

    The long run of extreme poverty

    Michail Moatsos estimates that about three-quarters of the world lived in extreme poverty in 1820. Our World in Data's count of people below $1.90 a day shows 965 million in 1820, 1,596 million in 1950, 1,904 million in 1990, 1,091 million in 2010 and 733 million in 2015.

    Method
    The 1820 share uses a cost-of-basic-needs approach, while later figures use an income or consumption line. The two do not measure the same thing.
    Count against share
    The count rose from 1820 to 1990 while the population also grew, and then fell.

    How solid The chart's own page names the $1.90 line but no data source and does not say how 1820–1980s values are made. The 1820 and later figures should be read as estimates with different methods.

  2. 1990 to 2026Extreme poverty

    Poverty at $3 a day

    The World Bank puts the number of people below $3 a day, in 2021 prices, at about 837 million in 2026, down from about 2.3 billion in 1990. Most of the decline came from East Asia and South Asia. Extreme poverty is increasingly concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and in places affected by conflict and fragility.

    Slowdown
    Progress has slowed over the last decade, and at the current pace ending extreme poverty could take decades.

    How solid From the World Bank's own page, which gives a different line and different prices from the $1.90 chart above, so the two series should not be joined. The page text states no global percentage. The Our World in Data author argues that a $3 line is too high to show the incomes of the very poorest.

  3. 1900 to 2025Income

    World output per person

    World output was 1,103 billion in 1900 and 4,082 billion in 1950 in 1990 international dollars on the DeLong reconstruction. Output per person was 11,275 in 1990 and 21,914 in 2025 in 2021 international dollars, and total output was about 180 trillion in 2025.

    Ratio
    By my division, output per person in 2025 was about 1.94 times that of 1990. The 1990–2025 per-person series has a different base from the DeLong series, so I do not join them.

    How solid Wikipedia's table gives no per-person figures before 1990. I found no world per-person series for 1825 or 1925.

Work and the home 2 entries

  1. 1870 to 2023Working hours

    Fewer hours of work

    Annual hours worked per worker fell in France from 3,168 in 1870 to 1,487 in 2023, in Germany from 3,284 to 1,335, in the United Kingdom from 2,755 to 1,523 and in the United States from 3,096 to 1,789. Japan fell from 2,076 in 1950 to 1,654, while India's figure was 2,434 in 1970 and 2,383 in 2023.

    Fall
    My division gives falls of 53% in France, 59% in Germany, 45% in the United Kingdom and 42% in the United States since 1870.
    Rich and poor
    Workers in richer countries tend to work fewer hours than those in poorer countries, and the page notes large inequalities within and between countries.

    How solid The page does not name the source of the series or say whether it counts full-time and part-time work alike, so the long-run values need checking before use.

  2. 1925 to 2020Comfort

    Light, heat and clean fuel at home

    Half of US households had electric power in 1925 and 90% by 1949. Worldwide, more than 1.5 billion people lacked electricity in 1998 and fewer than 1 in 10 lack it now. About two-thirds of the world cooked with clean fuels in 2020, up from about half in 2000, and in the United States nearly half of homes lacked complete plumbing in 1940 and 1% did in 1990.

    Detail
    The first chapter gives the full series and sources.

    How solid The US figures are from the OWID household-technology chart and the Census Bureau. The world figures are from Our World in Data's energy-access page, read through a summarizing tool.

Where the gains thin

These four limits come from the same sources as the entries above.

Supply is not intake

The FAO calorie supply of 3,006 a day is food available at retail, not food eaten, and it says nothing about vitamins and minerals.

Our World in Data

Hunger is concentrated and rising in places

In Africa the share facing hunger passed 20% in 2024, affecting 307 million people. The UN projects 512 million chronically undernourished by 2030, almost 60% in Africa.

WHO, from the UN report

Poverty progress has slowed

The World Bank says progress has slowed over the last decade and that ending extreme poverty at the current pace could take decades. The $3 line is itself argued to be too high to show the very poorest.

World Bank; Our World in Data

Not all workers gained time

India's annual hours per worker were 2,434 in 1970 and 2,383 in 2023, almost unchanged, while rich countries fell by 42–59% since 1870.

Our World in Data

What the record shows

These are my reading of the entries, not findings from the sources.

Supply rose, and hunger remains

The calorie supply per person rose by about 38% between 1961 and 2023 on my division (2,184 to 3,006), yet 8.2% of the world still faced hunger in 2024. On these numbers the problem is not the world average.

Deadly famine has become rare, and the earlier toll was large

The famine death rate fell to very low levels in the second half of the 20th century, and the one large recent case, Northeast Nigeria, is estimated at 340,000 deaths against tens of millions in earlier cases.

The number in extreme poverty fell by about 64% since 1990 on the World Bank figures

From about 2.3 billion to 837 million, my division, mostly in East and South Asia. The count had risen from 1820 to 1990, as population grew.

Working hours fell in the rich countries shown and hardly in India

The four rich countries shown cut annual hours by 42–59% since 1870. India's figure barely changed from 1970. Other countries are not shown, and the numbers do not show cause.

This chapter's data do not fall on 1825 and 1925

Calories begin in 1961, poverty counts in 1820 and 1950, the World Bank figure in 1990 and working hours in 1870. A split at 1925 cannot be tested here.

What this chapter cannot show

  • The measures cover food, money and hours, and say nothing about security, meaning or how people felt about their lives.
  • The poverty series use different lines, prices and methods, so the earlier and later figures should not be joined into one line.
  • I found no world per-person income series for 1825 or 1925. The only per-person series I read starts in 1990.
  • Several pages gave their data only in charts, which I read through downloaded data files and a summarizing tool. Check against the originals.
  • Housing beyond plumbing and electricity, clothing cost, and the price of food relative to wages are not covered here.
  • The list is a selection of eight entries, not a full account of material life.
