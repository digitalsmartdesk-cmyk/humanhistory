The question

This project asks what the last 100 years of advancement have done to humanity, including whether they left it more exposed to catastrophe than before. The chapters gather evidence on both sides, and only the sixteenth states and tests the hypothesis.

The hypothesis

Humanity has made itself vulnerable to catastrophic outcomes through the last 100 years of advancement, unlike any other 100 years of advancement in known history.

Tested in Chapter 16 against the evidence in the other chapters.

The chapters

Seventeen chapters in six parts. Each title links to its page.

Part 1 · The century 2 chapters

  1. Chapter 1

    The appearance of human life

    Six areas of daily life compared at about 1825, 1925 and 2025. The premise that life looked much the same in 1925 does not hold as stated for the United States, and holds better for parts of Asia and Africa. The biggest change after 1925 is in health and numbers (my reading).

  2. Chapter 2

    The pace of change

    The pace of change rose over two centuries, but the measures do not show a break at 1925: output stepped up near 1900, population after 1926 and energy use after 1950 (my reading).

Part 2 · Gains 3 chapters

  1. Chapter 3

    Life and health

    Children dying before 15 fell from about 1 in 2 to 4%, and life expectancy rose from 32 to 71 (1900–2021). Drug-resistant infection is one of the setbacks it records.

  2. Chapter 4

    Food, wealth and comfort

    Food supply rose from 2,184 to 3,006 kilocalories per person per day, and French working hours fell by more than half. 673 million still faced hunger in 2024.

  3. Chapter 5

    Knowledge and connection

    Adult literacy rose from about 1 in 10 people to about 88%, and internet use from 16% to 74%. 739 million adults lack basic literacy and 2.2 billion are offline.

Part 3 · Exposure 3 chapters

  1. Chapter 6

    A century of vulnerability

    Six features of exposure: planetary reach, speed, irreversibility, concentrated dependence, few hands and thin margins. 12,187 warheads, seven of nine planetary boundaries transgressed, and about half of food resting on synthetic nitrogen.

  2. Chapter 7

    The record of catastrophes

    Nineteen events. In share terms the worst losses came earlier: the Black Death took 30–60% of Europe, against 3.0–3.7% of the world in the Second World War.

  3. Chapter 8

    Defenses built

    Nineteen systems. Large measured falls where the hazard is known and countable, and several defenses weakening: New START expired, the US left the WHO, the pandemic annex is unfinished.

Part 4 · Beyond humans 3 chapters

  1. Chapter 9

    Nature at risk

    Monitored wildlife populations fell by an average of 73% from 1970 to 2022, about a million species are threatened, and 84% of reef area met bleaching-level heat in 2023–2025.

  2. Chapter 10

    Nature's catastrophes

    Mass extinctions removed about 70–85% of species at a time, and recovery took roughly 6 to 75 million years.

  3. Chapter 11

    Nature defended

    17.6% of land and 8.4% of ocean protected against 30% targets. Humpback whales recovered to about 93% of their pre-whaling size, and fewer than half of aligned national targets are on track.

Part 5 · How people live 4 chapters

  1. Chapter 12

    Work, inequality and population

    Child labour fell from 246 million to about 138 million. The richest 10% take 53% of income and the poorer half 8%. Population is projected to peak near 10.3 billion in the 2080s.

  2. Chapter 13

    Literacy, wisdom and knowledge

    Measured scores rose for decades and now show reversals in several countries. Wisdom has no agreed measure, and the chapter gives no verdict.

  3. Chapter 14

    Mind and wellbeing

    1.2 billion people live with a mental disorder, one third of those with depression get formal care, and 1 in 6 people are affected by loneliness.

  4. Chapter 15

    Power and governance

    By V-Dem's 2026 count 74% of people live in autocracies and democracy for the average person is back at its 1978 level. Freedom House reports 20 years of decline.

Part 6 · Weighing 2 chapters

  1. Chapter 16

    The comparison

    The hypothesis, stated and tested six ways. Realised loss: not supported. Worst case: supported as a model result only. Reach: supported in kind. Reversibility and defenses: mixed. Dependence: cannot be compared.

  2. Chapter 17

    What would change the balance

    Nine open items with their latest checked status and what to watch, from the pandemic annex and nuclear limits to a century-by-century tally nobody has yet built.

Where it stands

This is my reading of the chapters, not a finding from the sources.

The hypothesis is only partly borne out

The evidence does not show that this century has been the worst in realised loss. It does show exposure at the scale of the whole world that none of the 19 earlier events I selected matches.

The gains are large and counted

Health, food, literacy and connection improved by wide margins, and Chapters 3 to 5 give the figures. The strains in Chapters 12 to 15 remain alongside them.

The two sides are different kinds of evidence

Gains and past losses are counted. Much of the exposure is modelled or judged, so the two cannot be added into one balance.

A narrower claim fits better than the wording

The best-supported form is that this is the first century in my record in which human choices carry measured or modelled effects at the scale of the whole world. That is my judgment, and the original claim can still be kept and tested.

What this overview cannot show

  • Every chapter lists its own limits and the strength of each source, and those limits carry into this overview.
  • Most figures are secondhand: I read pages through a summarizing tool, so check figures against the originals before citing them.
  • Chapter 7 relies mostly on Wikipedia, and several later chapters rely on news reports of institutional reports.
  • The results in Chapters 16 and 17 are my judgment, not findings from the sources.
  • Chapters 12 to 15 rarely reach back to 1925, so for those the two centuries cannot be compared.
  • Figures are current to dates between 13 and 28 October 2026, and several items, such as nuclear limits and the pandemic annex, are still moving.
