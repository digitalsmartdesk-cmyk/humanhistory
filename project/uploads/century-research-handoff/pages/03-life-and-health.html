The gains

Eight entries in three groups. Each gives the scale, what drove it and how solid the figures are.

Surviving birth and childhood 2 entries

  1. About 1800 to 2023Child survival

    Children dying before they grow up

    Around two centuries ago, about 1 in 2 children died before the end of puberty. By 1950 the share had fallen to around one-quarter, and by 2020 to 4%. In 2023 the UN estimated 4.8 million children died before their fifth birthday, and said child deaths had dropped by more than half since 2000.

    Gaps
    In 2020, 13% of children in Nigeria and 7% in Pakistan died before 15, against 0.4% in Italy, Iceland, Japan and Norway.
    Reversal
    Child mortality rose in South Africa and Zimbabwe in the 1990s and 2000s, largely because of HIV/AIDS.
    Stillbirths
    About 1.9 million in 2023, down by over a third since 2000.

    How solid The 1800 figure is described only as 'around 1 in 2', with no exact value. The 1950 and 2020 figures are Our World in Data's. The 2023 count and the 'more than half since 2000' are from the UN's own estimate, as reported by WHO.

  2. 1800 to 2017Childbirth

    Mothers dying in childbirth

    In Sweden and Finland around 1800, around 900 mothers died per 100,000 live births, and in some countries in the 19th century the figure was roughly 500 to 1,000. In most rich countries it is now below 10, a fall of 50- to 100-fold.

    2017
    About 11 per 100,000 live births in high-income countries and about 450 in low-income countries, about 40 times higher.
    2015
    302,700 maternal deaths and about 140 million births, which I divide to about 216 per 100,000 as a rough world figure.
    Lowest
    Finland 3 and Sweden 4 per 100,000, with the year not given.

    How solid The page gives no global ratio, so the 216 figure is my own division of two numbers it does give. The figures for the 1800s are for a few countries and are not world figures.

Defeating particular diseases 4 entries

  1. 1967 to 1980Eradication

    Smallpox

    Donald Henderson estimates about 300 million people died of the disease in the 20th century. In 1967 an estimated 10 to 15 million people were infected each year while only 132,000 cases were reported. The last natural case of the milder form was in Merka, Somalia, on 26 October 1977, and the World Health Assembly certified elimination in May 1980.

    Last severe case
    The last case of the more severe form was in Bangladesh in October 1975.

    How solid The death toll is one expert's estimate. The page itself says reported cases were far below the real number, so the case figures for 1967 are estimates.

  2. 1955 to 2021Near-eradication

    Polio

    Salk's vaccine was announced as effective in April 1955 and Sabin's oral vaccine was licensed in 1961. In the early 1980s an estimated 300,000 to 400,000 people were paralysed each year, and polio was endemic in 125 countries in 1988. The page gives 1,873 estimated paralytic cases in 2020 and 649 reported cases in 2021.

    Where it remained
    Wild poliovirus cases were recorded only in Afghanistan, Pakistan and Malawi as of 2021.
    Fall
    The page states no percentage. My arithmetic gives a fall of about 99.4–99.5% from the early 1980s to 2020 in estimated cases.

    How solid The 1980s figure is an estimate and the 2021 figure is reported cases, so the two are not the same kind of count.

  3. 1974 to 2024Vaccination

    Routine childhood vaccination

    A 2024 study by Shattock and colleagues estimated that routine vaccinations prevented 154 million deaths worldwide in the last fifty years. Measles vaccination alone is credited with 94 million lives.

    Coverage
    Over 80% of infants worldwide now receive the full DTP course, a year not stated.
    Threshold
    Measles needs coverage typically above 90% for herd immunity.

    How solid These are modelled estimates, not counts. The page gives no period for the measles figure, though a related chart is titled 1974 to 2024, and it gives no coverage figures for earlier decades.

  4. 1900 to the 1990sInfection

    Pneumonia, tuberculosis and diarrhoea in the United States

    In 1900 pneumonia, tuberculosis and diarrhoea and enteritis were the leading causes of death, and with diphtheria they caused one-third of all deaths. In 1900, 194 of every 100,000 US residents died of tuberculosis.

    What changed it
    The CDC credits clean water, chlorination, sewage disposal and hygiene education, penicillin from the 1940s and later antimicrobials, and childhood vaccination programmes from the 1950s and 1960s.
    Not a straight line
    Infectious disease mortality rose overall during the 1980s and early 1990s.

    How solid From the CDC's account of 20th-century public health. It is about the United States only, and the page text gives no overall infectious disease death rate for 1900 or 1996.

Length of life 2 entries

  1. 1900 to 2023Life expectancy

    Living longer

    Global life expectancy at birth was 32 years in 1900 and 71 in 2021, according to Our World in Data, which says no region had a life expectancy above 40 in 1800. WHO puts it at 73.4 in 2019, 71.5 in the pandemic low of 2021 and 73.3 in 2023.

    Gap
    In 2021 life expectancy in Nigeria was thirty years lower than in Japan. Hong Kong had the highest female life expectancy, 88 years.
    Healthy years
    Healthy life expectancy was 62.8 in 2023, 0.4 years below its 2019 level.

    How solid The 1900 figure is a reconstruction and differs between sources (the earlier chapter shows 31 to 32 on one page). The 2023 figures are from a news report of WHO's October 2026 estimates.

  2. 2023Causes of death

    What people die of now

    Heart diseases cause a third of deaths worldwide and cancers almost one in five, together about every second death. Infectious diseases cause around 1 in 7. Neonatal and maternal deaths and accidents each account for about 4%, suicide for 1.3% and interpersonal violence for under 1%.

    Trend
    Death rates from both infectious and non-communicable diseases have fallen since 1990, communicable ones faster.

    How solid From the Global Burden of Disease via Our World in Data. The page gives no percentage for non-communicable diseases as a whole and no figures for how the shares shifted.

Where the gains thin

These four limits come from the same sources as the entries above.

Healthy years trail total years

Healthy life expectancy was 62.8 in 2023, against a life expectancy of 73.3. The difference is 10.5 years, my subtraction, and healthy life expectancy was still 0.4 years below its 2019 level.

WHO, via a news report

Resistance to antibiotics is growing

In 2021 an estimated 4.71 million deaths were associated with bacterial antimicrobial resistance and 1.14 million were attributable to it. The forecast for 2050 is 1.91 million attributable and 8.22 million associated deaths. Deaths in children under 5 fell by more than 50% from 1990 to 2021, while deaths in people over 70 rose by over 80%.

IHME, Lancet analysis

The gains are uneven

In 2020, 13% of children in Nigeria died before 15, against 0.4% in Italy. In 2021, life expectancy in Nigeria was thirty years lower than in Japan.

Our World in Data

Setbacks have occurred

The pandemic cut global life expectancy to 71.5 in 2021, and HIV/AIDS reversed child survival in South Africa and Zimbabwe for years. The UN warns that cuts in aid are putting decades of progress in child survival at risk.

WHO and Our World in Data

What the record shows

These are my reading of the entries, not findings from the sources.

A few named causes account for much of the change

The entries that carry the largest numbers are infections of childhood and birth: smallpox, polio, tuberculosis, diarrhoea and childbirth. The CDC and Our World in Data both attribute the decline to clean water, vaccines and antibiotics.

Several gains came from organised campaigns

Smallpox and polio were attacked by programmes with a target, and the 1980 certification of smallpox elimination is the one finished case in the record.

Most of the rise in life expectancy falls in the last 125 years

No region exceeded 40 in 1800, the world averaged 32 in 1900, and it was 71 in 2021. The chapter's data give no 1825 or 1925 figure, so the split at 1925 cannot be tested here.

The gains depend on a continuing system

Vaccination needs coverage above 90% for measles, antibiotics lose force as resistance grows, and the UN links aid cuts to disrupted vaccination and clinic closures.

The gaps between countries remain large

The sources show a 30-year gap in life expectancy between Nigeria and Japan in 2021, and in 2020 a share of children dying before 15 that was 13% in Nigeria against 0.4% in Italy, about 32 times higher by my division.

What this chapter cannot show

  • This chapter covers only what could be counted as survival and length of life. It does not measure pain, disability or the quality of care.
  • The deaths 'prevented' by vaccination and the deaths 'attributable' to resistance are model estimates, not counts.
  • Early figures are reconstructions. The 1800 and 1900 values rest on thin records, and several are given as ranges or as 'around'.
  • I did not find world figures for 1825 or 1925, so the two-century split used in earlier chapters cannot be applied here.
  • The list is a selection of eight gains. It leaves out insulin, anaesthesia, surgery, nutrition and many other advances.
  • Most figures were read through a summarizing tool from Our World in Data pages, which compile other sources. Check against the original studies before citing.
